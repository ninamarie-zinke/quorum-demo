Senolytic compound QZ-17 restores muscle regeneration in aged mice

Aged mice (24 months) received QZ-17 or vehicle for four weeks before a standard muscle injury model. Treated mice showed fewer senescent cells in muscle and larger regenerating fibers 14 days after injury. The authors propose that QZ-17 works by rescuing muscle stem cell function.

    Field expertiseCitation impact compared to others in the same field, so niche experts aren’t penalized
    Topic matchWhether the reviewer has published on this specific subject
    Review substanceEvidence cited, a specific claim targeted and a testable proposal

    Write a structured review

    Every review uses the same six fields, so authors and the AI pipeline never have to guess what a reviewer meant. Empty fields lower the substance score and show up as gaps.

    What this doesn’t guarantee
    
      
    Quorum ranks who to listen to first. It does not decide what is true.
    
    
    
    
      
      
    • Everything here is synthetic. Seed RCR, topic match and substance values are pre-set. In production they come from NIH iCite (RCR), reviewer abstracts via Paperclip (topic match) and Claude (substance).
      • 
      
    • New reviews get a structure score, not a judgment. In this demo, each filled evidence, specificity and hypothesis field adds 25 points, capped at 75. Claude’s substance review would replace this.
      • 
      
    • Votes measure popularity. They are displayed so readers see the crowd’s reaction, but they never move the credibility score. The self-test shows why.
      • 
      
    • RCR is still citation-based. It corrects for field size and career length, but well-known labs still get cited more, so a famous reviewer’s unsupported comment can still rank high.
      • 
      
    • Unverified reviewers are scored on their text alone. That lets a strong student rise, but a well-written wrong review could too. They are always tagged.
      • 
      
    • Author credit is the author’s call. Quorum records the decision and formats the asterisk. Journals set their own rules on what counts as authorship.
      • 
      
    • The self-test was written by our team. Real validation needs a hold-out set labeled by independent domain experts.
      • 
    
    
    
      
      
    • Credibility isn’t correctness. A well-cited reviewer can still be wrong, so read the evidence.
      • 
      
    • New reviewers are scored on their writing alone and are tagged as unverified.
      • 
      
    • Votes show popularity, not quality, which is why they don’t affect ranking.
      • 
      
    • Credit is the authors’ call. Journals set their own authorship rules.
